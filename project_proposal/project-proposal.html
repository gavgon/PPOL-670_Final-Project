Introduction

This project builds on Gavin Gondalwala’s proposal of analyzing the relationship between drought exposure and childhood stunting. The corresponding Github repo can be found at https://github.com/gavgon/PPOL-670_Final-Project.

Research Question & Motivation

Research Question

Does drought exposure predict child stunting in Sub-Saharan Africa?

Motivation

Climate change is disrupting weather patterns globally and increasing water scarcity. Simultaneously, global food aid is dwindling. Especially in countries/regions where subsistence farming is prevalent, we ask if there is a statistically significant association between drought exposure and childhood growth stunting, or if it could be attributed to other factors.

Data & Approach

Data

The following data sources will be used in the analysis:

  • DHS Surveys: Sourced from The Demographic and Health Surveys Program, microdata collected through household surveys has already been obtained which includes conventional demographic variables, in addition to menstrual data, food sources, education levels, food consumption, health data, and, notably, mother’s height. An additional application will be filed which, if granted, will allow the team to obtain GPS data for each household. Data is reported at the household level in separate national datasets which will be merged. While each national dataset is large, there are missing values for notable variables. For instance, Angola has roughly 32,000 missing observations for the child stunting variable, and Rwanda has roughly 22,000. Since the intention is aggregation across all of SSA, however, we believe there will still be an adequately powered sample. The data is downloadable as a .dta file, and there is also an R package called rdhs for manipulation.

  • SPEIbase: This is geographic data sourced from the Global SPEI database. The data covers land exposure to drought globally with 0.5 degrees of spatial resolution tracked monthly and is standardized. It contains variables for Standard Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index, latitude, longitude, and time, and there is a clear data dictionary. It can be accessed with the R package netcdf.

  • MapSPAM (IFPRI): SPAM uses inputs such as satellite crop coverage, land statistics, production & irrigation systems, crop intensity, crop suitability, rural population density, and crop prices to produce a dataset detailing hectares, farmed area, production, and yield. It has global coverage due to the satellite observations and is available as .csv files. It is at a granular grid cell level which is disaggregated from the broader units of countries and sub-national provinces.

Approach

The project aims to conduct a geospatial analysis with an econometric regression. The proposed form of the regression, with the variable of interest \(\beta_3\), is:

\[y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{stunted} + \beta_2 \text{drought} + \beta_3 (\text{stunt} \times \text{drought}) + \beta_4 (\text{land\_cultivation}) + \beta_5 (\text{mom\_height}) + \varepsilon\]

The current intention is to obtain GPS data from DHS and perform a point-polygon join to place households within drought grids from SPEIbase, which cross national borders, and within agricultural grids from MapSPAM. The underlying theory for including land under cultivation is that areas with little land under cultivation may not be impacted by drought since food is not sourced there; the theory underlying controlling for mother’s height is that a taller child may still be stunted when coming from a tall genetic background.

Upon further exploratory data analysis, additional control variables may be used.

Hurdles

There are two primary hurdles in this project which the team will have to navigate:

  1. GPS data availability: If DHS does not approve access to GPS data for the household surveys, we will not be able to do an analysis which is agnostic to borders. In the absence of GPS data, the drought data will be nationally aggregated and analysis will be conducted at the national level, instead of at a continuous spatial level.

  2. Creating a clear mapping between two different sets of polygons: SPEIbase and MapSPAM both have polygons, however at different resolutions. The team will need to find a coherent mapping between the two in order to get a clear analysis which includes drought conditions and land under cultivation. Should a clear mapping not prove possible, other nutrition and food consumption variables from the DHS surveys may be used in place of the MapSPAM data.
